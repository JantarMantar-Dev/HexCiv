Grove Redraw Study

The Grove tile redrawn from scratch in six different drawing techniques, not palette swaps. Each one is scored on the same checklist, and the winner's rules are written down so every other tile can follow them.

What was wrong with the current Grove

0stray single pixels

  1. The hex disappearsFive large crowns cover the whole top and spill past the edges, so the tile no longer reads as a hex.
  2. Trees merge into one massThe crowns overlap with no separation, so you can't count the trees or tell where one ends.
  3. Leaves are random noiseCrown shading is noise plus a scallop pattern, which leaves hundreds of stray pixels instead of leaf clusters.
  4. The light breaks into speckleThe noise term is as strong as the light term, so there's no clear lit side and shaded side on each tree.
  5. Outlines double upSoft and hard outlines from overlapping props land on top of each other and form thick dark seams.
  6. Details are scattered at randomFlowers and leaf flecks were placed by hash, so some landed under crowns, on edges or on the wall.
  7. The log reads as a barA flat brown strip with no cut end, so it doesn't read as a fallen log.
  8. The wall is noisy tooRandom pebbles, plates and vines make the side busier than the top, which pulls the eye down.

Six ways to draw it

Every candidate uses the same layout: four oaks, a bush, a fallen log, five flowers, four tufts and a mushroom. Nothing is random. Crowns are built from explicit clumps, facets or leaf stamps, the small props are hand-drawn sprites, and a cleanup pass removes any stray pixels left in the crowns and ground. The numbers are measured, not judged.

    The pick: Leafy Cluster

    It's the only style that passes every check. It keeps the scene's palette and soft lighting, so it drops into Verdant Isle without clashing, and its rules carry straight over to pines, bushes and the other tiles.

    Style rules to use on every tile

    • One light, upper leftEvery shape is lit from the same corner: lit caps top-left, dark crescents bottom-right.
    • Build from clumps, never noiseA crown is 5 lobed clumps, each shaded on its own. Random shading is not allowed.
    • Four tones per materialDark, mid, light and one highlight. The highlight only appears as small two-pixel leaf strokes.
    • Selective outlineOne pixel around each prop: deep teal on the bottom and right, pine on the top and left. Never doubled.
    • Keep the hex readableTrunks stand well inside the tile, and at least half of the top rim stays visible.
    • Hand-place detailsFlowers, tufts, logs and mushrooms are drawn sprites, placed on open ground and checked to stay on the top face.
    • Zero stray pixelsA cleanup pass merges any pixel that matches none of its neighbours. Only intended accents, like a flower's center, stay single.
    • Calm wallsEven strata bands, a fixed drip rhythm and a single crease, so the top stays the focus.
    Same 16-color palette throughout. Rendered live from code, so the numbers above are measured each time the page loads.
