Verdant Isle

256 × 192 px16 colors5 hex tileslight from upper left

A floating island of five hex tiles over a green valley: grove, pines, pond, meadow and mossy rocks. Every pixel is painted from one 16-color palette using the layering and color rules from Slynyrd's landscape backgrounds tutorial. Scrub the build steps to watch it come together layer by layer, or pick a tile below to see how it was built.

How each tile was built

Pick a tile, or click one on the scene above. Each panel adds one layer, the same back-to-front way the tutorial paints: shape, sides, texture, ground, props in two values, full shading, small details, then the tile in place.

    The tiles

    • A
      Grove

      Five broadleaf oaks of different sizes under one shaded floor, with undergrowth, a fallen log and a fern. Each crown is built from overlapping leaf clumps lit from the top left.

    • B
      Pines

      A stand of six conifers on a needle-dark floor, cooler and darker than the oaks, with arched ferns at their roots.

    • C
      Pond

      The water reuses the sky colors, with a dark far bank, tree reflections, lily pads and a bloom, cattails, bank stones, and a channel that spills off the island as a waterfall.

    • D
      Meadow

      Poppies, daisies, buttercups and lupines grow in single-kind clumps on the brightest grass. A dirt path narrows as it climbs between the back tiles, and two butterflies drift overhead.

    • E
      Rocks

      Angular boulders with flat facets, moss caps and cracks, a berry bush, pebbles, and red-cap mushrooms with white spots.

    Palette

    The sky top doubles as water and far mountain shadow. Mist covers the haze, the far mountains and the pond streaks. Shadows step down toward deep teal and never reach black.

    What the tutorial taught, and where it shows

    Tutorial ruleIn this scene
    Fix a console-friendly 4:3 canvas and a hard color cap256×192, which fits a 256×224 screen, and exactly 16 colors. The tutorial used 192×144 and 15 colors; five hexes needed the extra width.
    Start with flat horizon bars, then split them into bandsSteps 1 and 2. Band edges blend with short horizontal streaks instead of dithering.
    Depth through color: nearer is more saturated and contrasty, farther is lighter and bluerThe island holds the warmest greens and the darkest darks. The valley drops to sage and pine, and the mountains go blue.
    Hue-shift toward the sky with distanceThe grass ramp runs from deep teal through green and lime to warm yellow. Far planes take sage, then mist.
    Two mountain layers, the near one darker and bluer than the grass, with a haze band at its footSteps 3 and 4. A forested near range with tree-crown bumps sits on a mist band over the valley.
    Grass is 1px vertical streaks, scaled by distanceFront tiles get 3–4px blades, back tiles 1–2px clusters, the near valley short tufts, and the far valley none.
    Consistency in clustering and scaleOne noise field drives grass patches, flower clusters and leaf clumps, so they all clump the same way. Flowers are plus-shaped in front and single pixels in back.
    Layered clouds: the nearest has the most detail, the far ones are baked into the gradientPuffy near clouds drift among the far peaks. Far clouds are streaks inside the sky bands.
    Shadows are a darker, bluer shift of the local hueEvery cast shadow steps each color down one notch in its own ramp. Rocks and the underside shade into blue-grey.
    Clarity over detail, and every pixel should say somethingProps get a one-pixel outline: the darkest tone on the shaded bottom and right edges, a softer dark on the lit edges, so each asset reads against the grass. Tile seams are a dark groove with a lit bevel.
    Finish with a CRT pass at 4×The CRT toggle adds scanlines on the art grid, a slight color fringe and a vignette.
